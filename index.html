A blueprint for a
blue Arkansas.

We're moms and grandmas, nurses and neighbors — and we're done being quiet. Together.

We're not going to lie to y'all: some nights it feels like this state is gone. Then November 3rd happened. Arkansas flipped 10 seats in the state House — 30 out of 100 — and broke a supermajority that had run unchecked for ten years, which means no budget passes now without somebody having to talk to somebody. That's not a miracle. That's neighbors.

Arkansas's governor won with about 54% this time, down from 63%. Chris Jones lost his race by about 4,400 votes — while nearly a million registered Arkansans never voted at all. The majority is already here. It just hasn't all shown up on the same day yet.

So this is the plan — the Blueprint. Seventy-five counties. Every seat we can fill. One ride, one signature, one meeting at a time. Pull up a chair. There's a job here with your name on it, and we'll help you do it. 💙

The Moment

We didn't turn Arkansas blue on November 3rd. We proved we can.

Let's be honest about the scoreboard, because around here we deal in what's true:

30 of 100
Arkansas House seats now held by Democrats — 10 flipped on Nov 3, the party's largest gain in over 20 years.
75
Votes needed to pass an appropriations bill. Republicans have 70. For the first time since 2016, a budget requires Democratic votes — governing now requires talking.
~4,400
Votes that decided AR-02 against Chris Jones. A flip was that close.
~954,000
Registered Arkansans — about half — who did not vote. We don't need a miracle. We need our neighbors to show up.

What we didn't win — yet: the governor's mansion and the congressional seats, and Republicans still hold a supermajority in the state Senate (28–7, after Democrats flipped District 13). We say so plainly, because credibility is the brand — and because the plan below is built for exactly that gap: county by county, school board by school board, quorum court by quorum court, until showing up is just what Arkansas does.

Do

Three ways in — pick your job

1. Join your county

Change in Arkansas is built in county committees and local races. Find your county party, your next meeting, and the local seats — school board, quorum court, city council — coming open near you. Empty seats are how the other side kept power. We fill seats. Start with the Democratic Party of Lonoke County if you're local, and the Democratic Party of Arkansas for every other county.

2. Get trained

Our friends at Stand Up Arkansas recruit and train candidates and volunteers from precincts to school boards, all over this state. If you're thinking about running — or helping somebody who is — start there.

3. Run, help, or bring a friend

Not everybody runs for office. Somebody gathers the signatures. Somebody brings the casserole. Somebody drives a neighbor to early voting. Lia gathered about 90% of her son's ballot signatures while running her own race. Whatever your job is, we'll help you find it — start with the signup form.

How this started

Started by Lia Bell, the Blue Nurse in Arkansas

"I'm Lia Bell. I'm a mom, a grandma, and a nurse from Cabot, Arkansas — and I'm done being quiet."

I'm a registered nurse. I spent years as a school nurse here in Cabot, looking after other people's children like they were my own — because in a school, they are.

I'm a mom and a grandma. Everything I post comes from the same place: I want my grandkids to grow up in an Arkansas that takes care of people.

I didn't set out to be anybody on the internet. I started making videos because I was upset — about money being taken out of our public schools, about politicians who answer to donors instead of neighbors, about being told Arkansas will always be red so we shouldn't even try. 509 videos later, more than 7,000 of you are standing here with me.

I've done this work off-camera, too. In 2022 I ran for Cabot School Board myself, and I gathered about 90% of the signatures that put my son Corey on the ballot for City Council. Organizing, it turns out, is mostly showing up, knocking, asking, and not going away.

Blueprint Arkansas is the next step: turning all of us watching videos into all of us doing things — in every county in Arkansas. It isn't about me. It's about us, and it belongs to everybody who shows up.

— Lia Bell, @liabell71

Organizing runs in this family

Lia's son is Corey Spangler — Cabot City Council, Ward 1 Position 2, re-elected November 3, 2026, and chair of the Democratic Party of Lonoke County since 2021.

It started in 2022, when Lia talked him into running and then gathered about 90% of his ballot signatures herself. In Lonoke County he passed §601.4, a bylaw barring the county party and its candidates from taking money from entities the committee designates as grossly misaligned with its platform. On the council, he wrote Ordinance 33 of 2026 to bar city funds for third-party license-plate-reader systems, and Ordinance No. 21 of 2026, a first-of-its-kind religious exemption for compelled AI use at work.

Mom gathers the signatures. Son writes the bylaws. That's the model for the whole state: families, doing the unglamorous work, county by county.
